# SEC Marketing Rule — 17 CFR 275.206(4)-1 (Investment Advisers Act)

Effective compliance date Nov 4, 2022. Replaced the old Advertising and Cash Solicitation rules. A Utah state-registered adviser follows Utah/NASAA advertising rules, which mirror these principles — use the Marketing Rule as the conservative benchmark.

"Advertisement" is broad: any direct or indirect communication to more than one person (or one person if it includes hypothetical performance) that offers advisory services to prospects or new services to clients. **Websites, landing pages, emails, social posts, ads, webinars, and slide decks all count.**

## The seven general prohibitions
An advertisement may not:
1. Include an **untrue statement of a material fact**, or omit a material fact necessary to make a statement not misleading.
2. Include a **material statement of fact the adviser cannot substantiate** if the SEC demands it. (If you can't prove it on request, don't say it.)
3. Include information that would **reasonably likely cause an untrue or misleading implication or inference** about the adviser.
4. Discuss **potential benefits without fair and balanced treatment** of associated material risks or limitations.
5. Reference **specific investment advice** that is not presented in a fair and balanced manner.
6. Include or exclude **performance results, or present time periods, in a manner that is not fair and balanced.**
7. Be **otherwise materially misleading.**

## Testimonials and endorsements (now permitted, conditionally)
Requires:
- **Clear and prominent disclosure** (in the testimonial itself for the key items): whether the person is a **client/investor**, whether they are **compensated** (cash or non-cash), and **material conflicts of interest**.
- A **written agreement** with promoters who are compensated (above de minimis).
- **Adviser oversight** and compliance with the rule's conditions.
- **No disqualified persons** (certain bad-actor / disciplinary history).
- Third-party ratings need disclosure of the date, the entity, and whether compensation was paid.

## Performance advertising
- **Net of fees** required whenever gross performance is shown (with equal prominence).
- **Prescribed time periods**: 1-, 5-, and 10-year (or since inception) for non-private-fund performance.
- **Hypothetical performance** (incl. model/backtested/projected/targeted returns) requires policies & procedures, and providing the audience with information to understand criteria, assumptions, risks, and limitations — generally **not appropriate for a mass-market website**.
- **Related/extracted performance** must be fair and balanced; no cherry-picking profitable picks.

## Practical red flags for Capital Wealth web/email/ads
- Any number that looks like a return, projection, or dollar outcome → almost always a problem on a public page.
- Benefit bullets with no adjacent risk language → prohibition #4.
- "Our clients say…" carousels → testimonial disclosures required.
- "As seen on / award-winning / top advisor" → substantiate with source + date or remove.
- Anything implying the SEC/state endorses the firm → prohibited; pair registration mentions with "does not imply skill or training."
