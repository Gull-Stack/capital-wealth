# State insurance commissioner / NAIC advertising & suitability rules

Capital Wealth's affiliate **CWA Insurance Services, LLC** sells insurance/annuity products, regulated by state insurance commissioners under NAIC model regulations adopted by the states (Utah, Idaho, California, etc.). Review insurance/annuity content as a state insurance regulator would.

## Governing NAIC models
- **Model #570 — Advertisements of Life Insurance and Annuities.** Ads must not be deceptive or misleading; must not omit material information; must accurately describe policy/contract terms, guarantees, dividends (which are not guaranteed), and limitations. Source/identity of the insurer must be clear. Words like "investment," "profit," "savings" can't mislead about the insurance nature of the product.
- **Model #275 — Suitability and Best Interest in Annuity Transactions.** Producer must act in the consumer's **best interest** — obligations of **care, disclosure, conflict of interest, and documentation**. Recommendations must fit the consumer's financial situation, needs, and objectives.
- **Model #30 — Advertisements of Accident and Sickness Insurance** (relevant for any health/Medicare-adjacent content).

## Core advertising prohibitions (apply to web, email, ads, scripts)
- **No deceptive or misleading statements** about benefits, costs, terms, or the nature of the product.
- **Guarantees** may only be described as the **issuing insurer's contractual guarantees, subject to its claims-paying ability** — never as Capital Wealth's promise, never unqualified ("guaranteed income," "safe," "protected," "you can't lose").
- **Disclose material limitations** where a product is promoted: surrender charges and surrender period, fees and expenses, withdrawal restrictions, and that early withdrawal may incur charges/taxes.
- **No disparagement** of competitors, other products, or "Wall Street."
- **Identify insurance as insurance.** Don't disguise an annuity/insurance sale as neutral "financial education" or "planning" to lower the prospect's guard.
- **No misleading use of titles/designations** (e.g., "retirement specialist," "senior advisor") that imply special certification a producer doesn't hold; some states restrict senior-specific designations.

## Government-affiliation rule (overlaps with federal-employee marketing)
Insurance ads may not imply government affiliation or endorsement. For Capital Wealth's federal audience this reinforces the firm's own required disclaimer: **not affiliated with or endorsed by OPM, SSA, TSP, or any federal agency.** Government seals, flags, "official," CAGE/UEI badges, and Medicare/Social Security imagery must not imply the firm is a government program or government-endorsed.

## Practical red flags
- "Guaranteed income for life / safe money / protect your principal / tax-free retirement" → unqualified guarantee language.
- Annuity benefits listed with no mention of surrender charges/fees/carrier → omission of material info.
- "Better than a 401(k) / banks are lying to you" → disparagement + misleading comparison.
- Free-dinner / free-workshop framing that hides the sales purpose → must disclose products may be offered; keep "educational, no high-pressure pitch" honest.
